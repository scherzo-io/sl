```
/commercial/atrium-corporate-office/  prod 301 → /commercial/autrium-...-office/  new 404
/residential/vivvi-daycare/           prod 301 → /commercial/vivvi-daycare-tribeca/ new 404
/residential/mackage-soho/            prod 301 → /commercial/mackage-soho/        new 404
/residential/haus-nightclub/          prod 301 → /commercial/haus-nightclub/      new 404
```

Why it happens: WordPress's old-slug redirect matches on **slug**, ignoring the category segment.
Next.js matches on **exact path**. So every legacy URL whose category segment differs from the
project's current category resolves on WordPress and 404s here — and the 25 `REVIEW` rows were
classified as "needs a human target or an honest 404" without anyone checking that production
already answers all 25.

Three consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **`legacy-review-rows.md`'s premise is out of date.** The 25 targets are not unknown. Production
   supplies all 25, and `_wp_old_slug` in the committed WXR is the same mapping offline. Only two
   rows are genuinely a *judgment* call, and both are already in Eric's email: WP 564
   (`washington-sq-dermatology`, which production currently 301s **toward** `upper-east-side-townhouse`
   — the opposite of the planned fix) and WP 558 (`atrium` vs `autrium`).
2. **The gate's wording is itself wrong.** CLAUDE.md and `grok-build.md` §6 say "the 11 SKIP rows
   still serving 200". Only 4 do. The other 7 are 301→200, which satisfies the *intent* (never
   redirect a live slug away) while failing the letter. Checked literally at cutover this produces
   a false failure; checked loosely it hides the 404s above. It needs rewording either way.
3. `__trashed` → 410 (Grok's proposed row) would be a third instance of the same mistake:
   `/residential/upper-east-side-penthouse__trashed/` currently 301s to `/residential/e63-st-penthouse/`.

**Severity: blocker for cutover.** Not for the build — nothing here is badly written, and the fix
is data, not architecture.
